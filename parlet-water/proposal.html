What I heard

"The last thing I want this website to look like is sketchy."

Every sale in this business starts online. Your ads on Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube send people to one page, and that page has to do the job a salesman does for Culligan, without the salesman: turn a stranger into a quote request you can price and close. That is the whole pitch as you told it to me: you can beat their number by half because nobody drives out to the house to sell it, and you want the site to say so plainly.

The people clicking those ads are in their twenties and thirties and skeptical of everything that arrives through a feed. They need to land on something that is free of red flags, see real photos of you installing real systems, and find the quote button without scrolling, because in your words, if they have to hunt for it, half of them are gone. From there, four quick questions about their water and their household, and the lead is in your inbox. The current site was a favor that turned into a headache, and it looks like a lot of other websites. You want one that looks like nothing else.
